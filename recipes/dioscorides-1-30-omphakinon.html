Recipe

ὀμφάκινον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.30.1-2

Unripe-olive oil

draft

Omphakinon is raw-pressed olive oil, the best oil for health; the fresh, non-acrid, fragrant kind is also used for making perfumes. The only method in the passage is a stand-in for aged oil: the best oil to hand, boiled until it is as thick as honey.

TimeBoil until the oil has the consistency of honeyNo duration is given: the text names the point at which to stop.

Ingredients

Amount · metric

Olive oil to imitate aged oil

τοῦ παρόντος τὸ κρεῖσσον · ἔλαιον

the best oil available

no amount given

Method

  1. 1

    Imitate aged oil. If aged oil is not to be had, pour the best oil you have into a vessel and boil it until it is as thick as honey.

    No times

    The text gives no time or heat: only the thickness of honey to aim for.

  2. 2

    Use it. Use it as aged oil: it has the same capacity.

What to aim for

  • Raw-pressed oil is the best for health; the fresh, non-acrid, fragrant kind is better still, and is the kind used in making perfumes.
  • Boiled to the thickness of honey, the best oil to hand has the same capacity as aged oil.

Uses

Raw-pressed oil: good for the stomach, held in the mouth for the gums and teeth, checks sweating; the richer, aged oil for relaxing remedies. All oil warms and softens the flesh; it eases the bowels, is drunk and vomited against poisons, purges taken with barley-gruel liquid or water, is given boiled with rue for griping and worms, and by enema for obstruction; aged oil is more warming and sharpens the sight.

Greek · Wellmann

ἔλαιον πρὸς τὴν ἐν ὑγιείᾳ χρῆσιν ἄριστον τὸ ὠμοτριβές, ὃ καὶ ὀμφάκινον καλοῦσι. καὶ τούτου διαφέρει τὸ νέον, ἄδηκτον, εὐῶδες· χρήσιμον δὲ τὸ τοιοῦτον καὶ εἰς τὴν τῶν μυρων κατασκευήν. ἔστι δὲ καὶ εὐστόμαχον διὰ τὸ στύφε ειν καὶ οὔλων σταλτικὸν καὶ ὀδόντων διακρατυντικὸν κρατούμενον ἐν τῷ στόματι ἱδρώτων τε ἐφεκτικόν. τὸ δὲ λιπαρώτερον καὶ παλαιὸν εἰς τὴν τῶν χαλαστικῶν φαρμάκων χρῆσιν εὔθετον. κοινῶς δὲ πᾶν ἔλαιον θερμαντικόν ἐστι καὶ μαλακτικὸν τῆς σαρκός, δυσπερίψυκτα φυλάσσον τὰ σώματα καὶ ἑτοιμότερα πρὸς τὰς ἐνεργείας ποιοῦν. 2 ἔστι δὲ καὶ εὐκοίλιον καὶ μαλακτικὸν ἀμβλῦνόν τε τὰς τῶν ἑλκούντων φαρμάκων δυνάμεις· δίδοται δὲ καὶ πρὸς τὰ θανάσιμα συνεχῶς πινόμενον καὶ ἐξεμούμενον. καθαίρει δὲ ὅσον κοτύλη σὺν πτισάνης χυλῷ ἴσῳ ποθὲν ἢ ὕδατι, στροφουμένοις τε ἐν ποτῷ ὠφελίμως δίδοται μετὰ πηγάνου ἑψηθὲν θερμὸν κυάθων ἓξ πλῆθος ἕλμεις τε ἐκβάλλει, ἐνίεταί τε τὸ αὐτὸ μάλιστα πρὸς τοὺς εἰλεώδεις. τὸ δὲ παλαιὸν θερμαντικώτερον καὶ διαφορητικώτερον, πρὸς ὀξυδερκίαν τε ὑπάλειμμα. μιμητέον δὲ εἰ μὴ παρείη τὸ παλαιὸν ἔλαιον οὕτως· κατεράσας εἰς ἀγγεῖον τοῦ παρόντος τὸ κρεῖσσον ἕψε, ἕως οὗ σχῇ μέλιτος πάχος, καὶ χρῶ· τὴν γὰρ αὐτὴν τῷ προειρημένῳ εἰσφέρεται δύναμιν.

English · approved translation

1. For maintaining health, raw-pressed oil—the kind also called omphakinon—is best. Among such oils, the fresh, non-acrid, fragrant kind is superior; oil of this sort is also useful in preparing perfumes. Its astringency makes it beneficial to the stomach. When held in the mouth, it contracts the gums and strengthens the teeth, and it also checks perspiration. The more unctuous, aged oil is suitable for use in relaxing remedies. In general, every olive oil warms and softens the flesh, protects the body from being readily chilled, and makes it readier for activity.

2. It also makes evacuation easy and softens the bowels, while blunting the capacities of medicines that cause ulceration. It is administered against deadly poisons by repeatedly drinking it and vomiting it back up. It produces a purge when a cotyle is taken with an equal measure of barley-gruel liquid or water. For people suffering intestinal griping, six cyathoi boiled with rue are beneficially given warm as a drink. This also expels intestinal worms, and the same preparation is administered by enema, especially in cases of intestinal obstruction. Aged oil is more warming and more dispersive, and it is used as an ointment to sharpen vision. If aged oil is unavailable, it should be imitated as follows: pour the best available oil into a vessel and boil it until it acquires the consistency of honey, then use it, since it supplies the same capacity as the oil described above.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.30.1-2, ed. Wellmann.

  • Flag perfume-tables gives the locator as 1.30, overlapping the three later recipes of the chapter; cut to 1.30.1-2. The only procedure in it is the imitation of aged oil, not a preparation of omphakinon.

Editor's notes

Chapter 1.30 holds four recipes: this one (1.30.1-2), oil of the wild olive (1.30.3, dioscorides-1-30-3-agrias-elaias), whitened oil (1.30.3-4, dioscorides-1-30-whitened-oil) and Sicyonian oil (1.30.5, dioscorides-1-30-5-sikyonion). The cut ends before 3 τὸ δὲ ἐκ τῆς ἀγρίας ἐλαίας.

Omphakinon itself is only defined here (raw-pressed oil, ὠμοτριβές); the one procedure in the passage is the imitation of aged oil, which the steps give.

The doses in the uses passage (a cotyle of oil as a purge, six cyathoi boiled with rue) are of the oil itself and are not listed as ingredients.

Section 6 of the chapter (grime from baths, palaestra, gymnasium walls and statues) belongs to none of the four recipes and is left out of all of them.
