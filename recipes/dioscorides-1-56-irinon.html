Iris unguent

draft · Iris unguent

g of olive oil the written batch

Iris unguent. Oil is first made astringent and fragrant by boiling it with finely chopped palm spathe and water in a bronze vessel, and strained into a honey-coated bowl. Iris is steeped in this oil to make the first iris unguent; the steeping itself is set out only in the other method of the chapter.

Method

  1. 1

    Prepare the oil. Chop 6 lb 8 oz of palm spathe as finely as possible. Mix it with 9 lb 5 oz of oil and 10 kotylai of water, put it into a bronze vessel, and boil until the oil takes on the scent.

  2. 2

    Strain. Strain it into a mixing bowl coated with honey.

  3. 3

    Steep the iris. Steep iris in this prepared oil to make the first iris unguent.

    As set out below

    The text points ahead. The only steeping of iris it describes, with amounts and times, is the other method of section 2.

What to aim for

  • The best smells of iris alone and of nothing else.
  • Such is the iris unguent made at Perge in Pamphylia and at Elis in Achaea.

Uses

Softening and warming: cleanses eschars, putrefaction and filth; for the womb, its inflammations and closures; expels embryos and opens haemorrhoids; with vinegar, rue and bitter almonds for sounds in the ears; for chronic catarrh and ozena; a kyathos drunk purges the bowels, helps ileus and moves urine; for those who vomit with difficulty, for synanche (gargled with melikraton), a rough windpipe, and after hemlock, mushrooms or coriander.

Greek · Wellmann

ἰρίνου στύψις· σπάθης λίτρας ἓξ οὐγγίας ὀκτώ, ἐλαίου λίτρας ἐννέα οὐγγίας πέντε κόψας ὡς λεπτότατα μεῖξον ὕδατος κοτύλαις δέκα καὶ βαλὼν εἰς χαλκὸν ἓψε, μέχρις οὗ τὴν ὀσμὴν ἀναλάβῃ, εἶτα ἀπήθει εἰς κρατῆρα μέλιτι κατακεχρισμένον. ἐκ τούτου τοῦ ἠρωματισμένου ἐλαίου τὸ πρῶτον ἴρινον σκευάζεται ἐναποβρεχομένης τῷ ἐστυμμένῳ ἐλαίῳ ἴρεως, ὡς ὑπογέγραπται. […] 3 κράτιστον δὲ ὑπάρχει τὸ ἐν τῇ ἀσφρήσει μηδενὸς ἄλλου, μόνης δὲ τῆς ἴριδος πνέον. τοιοῦτον δέ ἐστι τὸ ἐν Πέργῃ τῇς Παμσφυλίας καὶ ἐν Ἤλιδι τῆς Ἀχαίας γινόμενον. δύναμιν δὲ ἔχει μαλακτικήν, θερμαντικήν, ἐσχάρας τε καὶ σηπεδόνας καὶ ῥυπαρίας ἀνακαθαίρει καὶ ταῖς περὶ μήτραν διαθέσεσιν ἁρμόζει, φλεγμοναῖς τε καὶ μύσεσιν, ἔμβρυά τε ἐκβάλλει καὶ αἱμορροίδας ἀναστομοῖ, πρός τε ἤχους ὤτων σὺν ὄξει καὶ πηγάνῳ καὶ πικροῖς ἀμυγδάλοις, καὶ πρὸς κατάρρους χρονίζοντας διαχριομένων τῶν μυξωτήρων καὶ πρὸς ὀζαίνας. 4 καθαίρει δὲ καὶ τὴν κοιλίαν κυάθου πλῆθος ποθέν, ποιεῖ καὶ πρὸς εἰλεώδη, κινεῖ καὶ οὖρα. ἁρμόζει καὶ ἐπὶ τῶν δυσχερῶς ἐμούντων διαχριομένων τῶν δακτύλων ἢ τῶν ἐμετηρίων, καὶ ἐπὶ συναγχικῶν δὲ διαχριόμενον ἢ ἀναγαργαριζόμενον σὺν μελικράτῳ καὶ πρὸς τραχύτητα ἀρτηρίας. δίδοται καὶ τοῖς κώνειον ἢ μύκητας ἢ κόριον πεπωκόσιν.

English · approved translation

1. Astringency of iris unguent. Chop six litrai and eight oungiai of spathe as finely as possible. Mix it with nine litrai and five oungiai of oil and ten kotylai of water, put the mixture into a bronze vessel, and boil it until the oil takes on the fragrance. Then strain it into a mixing bowl coated with honey. From this aromatized oil the first iris unguent is prepared by steeping iris in the astringent oil, as set out below. […] 3. In fragrance, the best kind is that which smells of iris alone and of nothing else. Such is the kind made at Perge in Pamphylia and at Elis in Achaea. It has an emollient and warming capacity. It thoroughly cleanses eschars, putrefactions, and filth, and is suitable for conditions affecting the uterus, including inflammations and closures. It also expels embryos and opens hemorrhoids. For sounds heard in the ears, it is used together with vinegar, rue, and bitter almonds. It is also suitable for persistent catarrhs, when the nostrils are anointed with it, and for ozena.

4. When one kyathos is drunk, it also purges the bowels, helps in ileus-like conditions, and promotes urination. It is also suitable for those who vomit only with difficulty, when either the fingers or the emetic agents are smeared with it; for those suffering from synanche, when it is applied as an ointment or gargled with melikraton; and for roughness of the windpipe. It is also administered when hemlock, mushrooms, or coriander have been swallowed.

Source
