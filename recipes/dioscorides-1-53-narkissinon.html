ναρκίσσινον narkissinon

Narcissus unguent

Narcissus unguent family7 ingredientsTime · boil, steep

g of ἐλαίου (oil) as written

Narcissus unguent is made from narcissus flowers steeped in washed oil first made astringent with aspalathos, calamus and myrrh. The oil is boiled with aspalathos, then with calamus and myrrh worked in fragrant old wine, cooled and strained; some of it is poured over as many narcissus flowers as possible, stirred for two days and pressed out. The unguent is decanted often, because Dioscorides says that it too spoils easily.

Time

Boil
twice, with the aspalathos and then with calamus and myrrh; no duration given
Steep
two days, stirring

The oil is cooled before straining. No temperatures are given.

Method

  1. Boil the aspalathos Chop 6 lb 2 oz (2 kg) of aspalathos and soak it in water.

    Boil it with a third of 9 lb 5 oz (3–3.1 kg) of washed oil.

    Take out the aspalathos.

  2. Boil with calamus and myrrh Pound and sift 5 lb 8 oz (1.8–1.9 kg) of calamus with a lump of myrrh.

    Work them with fragrant old wine.

    Mix them into the oil and boil again.

  3. Cool and strain Take it off the heat once it has boiled up.

    Let it cool.

    Strain the oil.

  4. Steep the narcissus Pour some of this oil into a basin and add as many narcissus flowers as you can.

    Stir for two days, as for lily oil (1.52).

  5. Press and decant Press it out.

    Decant it often.

    Why often Dioscorides says this unguent too spoils easily, like lily oil (1.52).

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides warns that it spoils easily and should be decanted often.
