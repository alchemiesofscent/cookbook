Narcissus unguent

draft · Narcissus unguent

g of olive oil the written batch

Washed oil is made astringent by boiling: first with aspalathos, then with calamus and myrrh worked in old wine; it is cooled and strained. Some of this oil is poured into a basin with as many narcissus flowers as it will take, stirred for two days, pressed out and decanted often, since it spoils easily.

TimeBoil twice, with the aspalathos and then with calamus and myrrh; no duration givenSteep two days, stirringThe oil is cooled before straining. No temperatures are given.

Method

  1. 1

    Boil the aspalathos. Chop 6 lb 2 oz of aspalathos and soak it in water. Mix it with a third of 9 lb 5 oz of washed oil and boil. Then take the aspalathos out.

  2. 2

    Boil with calamus and myrrh. Pound and sift 5 lb 8 oz of calamus with a lump of myrrh, work them with fragrant old wine, mix them into the oil, and boil again.

    The rest of the oil

    The text does not say when the other two-thirds of the oil go in; it says only “into the oil”.

  3. 3

    Cool and strain. When it has boiled up with these as well, take it off the heat, let it cool, and strain the oil.

  4. 4

    Steep the narcissus. Pour some of this oil into a basin and put in as many narcissus flowers as you can. Stir for two days, as for lily oil (1.52).

    How much oil

    The amount is lost in a gap (lacuna) in the Greek, marked in the approved English.

  5. 5

    Press and decant. Press it out and decant it often, for this oil too spoils easily.

    This too

    Like lily oil (1.52), which spoils if it is left with the water pressed out with it.

What to aim for

  • It spoils easily: decant it often.

Uses

For disorders of the uterus, softening the indurations and closures around it; but it causes headache.

Greek · Wellmann

ναρκίσσινον δὲ στύψεις οὕτως· ἐλαίου πεπλυμένου λίτρας ἐννέα οὐγγίας πέντε, ἀσπαλάθου λίτρας ἓξ οὐγγίας δύο κόψας καὶ βρέξας ὕδατι. τῷ τρίτῳ τοῦ ἐλαίου μείξας ἕψε, εἶτα ἐξελὼν τὸν ἀσπάλαθον καὶ καλάμου λίτρας πέντε οὐγγίας ὀκτὼ καὶ σμύρνης χόνδρον κόψας καὶ σήσας φύρα οἴνῳ παλαιῷ εὐώδει καὶ μείξας ἕψε. ὅταν δὲ καὶ μετὰ τούτων ἀναζέσῃ, ἐξελὼν ἀπήθει τὸ ἔλαιον ψυγέν, εἶτα λαβὼν ἐκ τοῦ ἐλαίου [lacuna] [lacuna] εἰς λουτῆρα ἀπόχει καὶ τοῦ ναρκίσσου ἔμβαλε ὡς ὅτι πλεῖστον τὸ ἄνθος, ἐπὶ ἡμέρας δύο κινῶν ὡς ἐπὶ τοῦ σουσίνου, ἐξίπου τε καὶ μετάγγιζε πυκνῶς· καὶ γὰρ τοῦτο σήπεται. ποιεῖ δὲ πρὸς τὰ ἐν ὑστέρᾳ, μαλάσσον τὰς περὶ αὐτὴν σκληρίας καὶ μύσεις. ἔστι δὲ κεφαλαλγές.

English · approved translation

Prepare the astringent base for narcissus unguent as follows. Take nine litrai and five ounces of washed oil, and six litrai and two ounces of aspalathos, chopped and soaked in water. Combine the aspalathos with a third of the oil and subject it to boiling. Then remove the aspalathos. Pound and sieve five litrai and eight ounces of calamus together with a lump of myrrh; work them with fragrant old wine, incorporate the mixture into the oil, and boil it. When the oil has boiled up with these ingredients as well, remove it from the heat and strain it after it has cooled. Next, take from the oil [lacuna] [lacuna], decant it into a basin, and put in the greatest possible quantity of narcissus blossom. Stir for two days, as with lily unguent, then press it out and decant it frequently, for this too is liable to spoil. It is effective for disorders of the uterus, softening the indurations and closures around it; but it causes headache.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.53, ed. Wellmann.
