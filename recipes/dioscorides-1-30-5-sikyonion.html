Sicyonian oil

draft · Sicyonian oil

ml of olive oil the written batch

Sicyonian oil, made chiefly in Sicyonia, is fresh, pale, raw-pressed olive oil boiled with water. It is brought to the boil twice over a gentle fire, cooled, and the oil ladled off; then it is boiled again with more water, finished like the oil described before it, and stored.

Method

  1. 1

    Boil with water. Pour 1 chous of fresh, pale, raw-pressed oil and ½ chous of water into a wide-mouthed, tin-lined cauldron. Boil it over a gentle fire, stirring slowly.

  2. 2

    Cool and ladle off. When it has boiled up twice, take away the fire. When it has cooled, ladle off the oil.

  3. 3

    Boil again. Add more water and bring it to the boil again with the oil.

    More water

    No amount is given.

  4. 4

    Finish and store. Do the rest as described above, and store it.

    As described above

    The text points back to the oil before it, the whitened oil (1.30.3–4), presumably its sunning and storage; it does not say which steps.

Uses

Moderately warming: for fevers and afflictions of the sinews; women also use it [on the face] for a sheen.

Greek · Wellmann

ἔνεστι δὲ καὶ Σικυώνιον διὰ τοῦ προειρημένου οὕτως 5 σκευάζειν· εἰς λέβητα κεκασσιτερωμένον πλατύστομον ἐγχέας νέου ἐλαίου ὠμοτριβοῦς καὶ λευκοῦ χοέα ἕνα καὶ ὕδατος ἡμίχουν ἕψε κούφῳ πυρὶ κινῶν πραέως. ὅταν δὲ ἀναζέσῃ δίς, ὑπόσπασον τὸ πῦρ καὶ ψύξας ἀποκόγχισον τὸ ἔλαιον, εἶτα ἄλλο ἐπιδούς ὕδωρ συνανάζεσον αὐτῷ, καὶ τὰ λοιπὰ ποιήσας καθὼς ὑποδέδεικται ἀπόθου. σκευάζεται δὲ τοῦτο μάλιστα ἐν Σικυωνίᾳ, Σικυώνιον καλούμενον. δύναμιν δὲ ἔχει ποσῶς θερμαντικήν, ἁρμόζον ἐν πυρετοῖς καὶ τοῖς περὶ νεῦρα πάθεσι. χρῶνται δὲ αὐτῷ καὶ αἱ γυναῖκες [ἐπὶ τῷ προσώπῳ] ἐπὶ τῷ στιλβώθρῳ.

English · approved translation

5. Sicyonian oil can also be prepared from the oil described above as follows. Pour one chous of fresh, pale, raw-pressed oil and half a chous of water into a wide-mouthed, tin-lined cauldron. Boil it over a gentle fire, stirring slowly. Once it has boiled up twice, withdraw the fire from beneath it; when it has cooled, ladle off the oil. Then add more water, bring it to the boil again together with the oil, perform the remaining steps as indicated above, and store it. This oil is prepared chiefly in Sicyonia and is called Sicyonian oil. It has a moderately warming capacity and is suitable in fevers and afflictions involving the sinews. Women also use it [on the face] to produce a sheen.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.30.5, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The cut runs from ἔνεστι δὲ καὶ Σικυώνιον to the end of section 5. Wellmann prints the section number 5 inside the first sentence (διὰ τοῦ προειρημένου οὕτως 5 σκευάζειν); section 5 really begins at ἔνεστι δὲ.

"From the oil described above" (διὰ τοῦ προειρημένου) and "the remaining steps as indicated above" (τὰ λοιπὰ … καθὼς ὑποδέδεικται) point back to the whitened oil (dioscorides-1-30-whitened-oil), presumably its sunning and storage.

The Greek keeps Wellmann's bracketed words [ἐπὶ τῷ προσώπῳ], the English "[on the face]".

Reader pass: the water is moved to the working materials, since the oil is ladled off it; the second water (ἄλλο ὕδωρ, no amount) is folded into the first.
