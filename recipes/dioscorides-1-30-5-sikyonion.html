σικυώνιον sikyonion

Sicyonian oil

Sicyonian oil family1 ingredientTime · boil

ml of ἐλαίου (oil) as written

Sicyonian oil is fresh, pale, raw-pressed olive oil boiled with water in a tin-lined cauldron. It is brought to the boil twice over a gentle fire, cooled and ladled off, then boiled again with more water, finished as the whitened oil (1.30.3–4) and stored. Dioscorides says it is made chiefly in Sicyonia and named for it, and that women use it for a sheen on the face.

Metric figures are ranges, from the pound (λίτρα) of 322–329 g and the other units; the scaler changes them and leaves the ancient amounts as written.

Method

  1. Boil with water Pour 1 chous (3.3 l) of fresh, pale, raw-pressed oil and ½ chous (1.6 l) of water into a wide-mouthed, tin-lined cauldron, and boil it over a gentle fire, stirring slowly.

  2. Cool and ladle off Take away the fire once it has boiled up twice, let it cool, and ladle off the oil.

  3. Boil again Add more water and bring it to the boil again with the oil.

  4. Finish and store Finish it as for the whitened oil (1.30.3–4), and store it.

    The finish Probably the sunning, and storage over melilot and iris, of the whitened oil.
