Lily oil

draft · Lily perfume

g of olive oil the written batch

Oil is first made astringent: boiled with calamus and myrrh in fragrant wine, strained, then steeped with soaked cardamom and pressed. The petals of a thousand lilies are steeped in it for a day and a night and pressed, and the oil is at once separated from the water and decanted with salt. The same petals are pressed twice more with fresh oil and cardamom for lesser grades; fresh lilies make the oil stronger, and it is finished with myrrh, cinnamon and saffron.

TimeSteep the lilies a day and a night; press in the morningRest a short while, before the second pressing; the third is done the same wayThe boil and the steeping with cardamom have no stated times. No temperatures are given.

Method

  1. 1

    Make the astringent oil. Mix 9 lb 5 oz of oil, 5 lb 10 oz of calamus and 5 drachmas of myrrh with fragrant wine and boil them. Strain off the oil.

  2. 2

    Steep the cardamom. Chop 3 lb 6 oz of cardamom and soak it in rainwater. Pour the strained oil over it, let it steep, and press it out.

    Made astringent

    The Greek calls this oil “made astringent” (ἐστυμμένον) when it is used next; the English says “expressed”. Saffron oil (1.54) starts from the same oil.

  3. 3

    Steep the lilies. Strip the petals from a thousand lilies and lay them in a broad, shallow basin. Pour 3½ lb of the prepared oil over them and stir with hands coated with honey. Leave them for a day and a night.

    Broad, not deep

    The petals lie spread out; the text says “spread them out” of the second thousand.

  4. 4

    Press, separate, decant. In the morning put them in a straining basket and press them out. At once separate the oil from the water pressed out with it: unlike rose oil, this oil does not tolerate the water, and warmed with it, it bubbles up and spoils.

    Decant it several times into other vessels coated with honey, sprinkling on fine salt and carefully taking off the impurities that collect. This first expression is the finest.

    The water

    Presumably the lilies’ own juice, like the rose juice that spoils rose oil (1.43).

  5. 5

    Press the petals again. Put the pressed petals from the basket back in the basin. Pour on the same amount of aromatised oil as before and add 10 holkai of chopped cardamom. Work it well with your hands, wait a short while, and press it out, cleaning what runs off. This second expression is second in quality.

    Pour oil over the petals a third time and do the same, again adding cardamom and salt, coating your hands with honey, and pressing. This last expression is third.

    Holkai

    The holke is the drachma weight: 10 holkai are 10 drachmas.

  6. 6

    A stronger oil. Strip another thousand lilies and spread out the petals. Pour the first expression over them and treat them just as the first, mixing in cardamom; then press them out. Treat the second and third expressions the same way, with cardamom. The more often you steep fresh lilies in it, the stronger the perfume.

    Two kinds of repeat

    Pressing the same petals again (step 5) gives lower grades; fresh lilies steeped in the first expression (step 6) make it stronger.

  7. 7

    Finish with spices. At the end, when it seems strong enough, mix into each preparation 2 drachmas of good myrrh, 2 drachmas of cinnamon and 10 drachmas of saffron.

    Each preparation

    The text does not say whether this means each grade or each batch.

Other ways

A different finish. Others pound and sift equal amounts of saffron and cinnamon and put them in the basin with water. They pour the first expression over them, leave it a short while, and take it up into dry vessels coated beforehand with gum, myrrh, saffron, and honey thinned with water. They do the same with the second and third expressions.

No amounts are given; the gum, myrrh, saffron and honey only coat the vessels.

What to aim for

  • The first expression is the finest, the next second, the last third.
  • The more often fresh lilies are steeped in it, the stronger the perfume.
  • Left with the water pressed out with it, the oil bubbles up when warmed and spoils: separate it at once.

English · approved translation

1. Preparation of susinon, which some call leirinon. Mix nine litrai and five ounces of oil, five litrai and ten ounces of calamus, and five drachmai of myrrh with fragrant wine, and boil them. Then filter the oil and pour it over three litrai and six ounces of cardamom that has been chopped and soaked in rainwater. Let it steep, then press it out. Take three and a half litrai of the expressed oil; strip the petals from one thousand lilies and place them in a broad, shallow basin. Pour the oil over them and stir with hands previously coated with honey. Leave them for one day and one night; in the morning, transfer them to a straining basket and press them out.

2. Immediately filter the liquid that comes away, separating the oil from the water expressed with it. Unlike rose perfumed oil, this oil does not tolerate the water: when heated together with it, it bubbles up and spoils. Decant it several times into other vessels previously coated with honey, sprinkling it with finely ground salt and carefully removing the impurities that collect. Transfer the pressed aromatic material from the straining basket back into the basin. Pour over it the same quantity of aromatized oil as before and add ten holkai of chopped cardamom. Work it thoroughly with your hands, wait a short while, and press it out, removing impurities from the liquid that runs off.

3. Pour aromatized oil over the material a third time and repeat the same procedure, again adding the cardamom and the salt, coating your hands with honey, and pressing. The first expression will be the finest, the next one second in quality, and the last one third. Then take another thousand lilies, strip off their petals, and spread them out. Pour the first expression over them and process them just as you did the first set, mixing in cardamom, and then press them out. Treat the second and third expressions in the same way, as stated, adding cardamom as well. The more often you steep fresh lilies in it, the stronger your perfumed oil will become.

4. Finally, when you think it is sufficiently strong, mix into each preparation two drachmai of good myrrh, two drachmai of cinnamon, and ten drachmai of saffron. Others proceed as follows: pound and sift equal quantities of saffron and cinnamon, put them into the basin with water, and pour the first expression of perfumed oil over them. After leaving it briefly, transfer it into dry vessels previously coated with gum, myrrh, saffron, and honey thinned with water. Do the same with the second and third expressions.

Editor's notes

The first expression is the finest, the next second, the last third. The more often fresh lilies are steeped in it, the stronger the perfume.

The Greek ends before ἔνιοι δὲ τὸ ἁπλοῦν: the simple form that follows is a separate recipe (dioscorides-1-52-5-sousinon-haploun).

The coating of the others' storage vessels (gum, myrrh, saffron, honey thinned with water) is given under Other ways and not listed among the ingredients.

The plain steps are regrouped into seven titled steps; the others' finishing method (the last plain step) is moved to Other ways.

Rainwater and honey are moved to the working materials.

The Greek calls the 3½ litrai taken for the lilies "astringed oil" (ἐκ τοῦ ἐστυμμένου ἐλαίου); the English says "the expressed oil".
