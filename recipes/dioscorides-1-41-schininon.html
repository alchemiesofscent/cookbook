σχίνινον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.41

Mastic-tree oil

draft · Mastic-tree oil

Made in the same way as bay oil, from the ripe fruit of the mastic tree; or else made astringent by the bay-oil method. The text gives no further detail. Terebinth oil is made the same way.

Ingredients

Amount · metric

Mastic-tree fruit

σχίνου · σχῖνος

The ripe fruit.

no amount given

Method

  1. 1

    Make it like bay oil. Prepare the ripe fruit of the mastic tree in the same way as bay oil.

    In the same way

    Bay oil (1.40) is made by boiling the ripe berries in water and gathering the oily substance their skins release.

Other ways

Made astringent. Or else make it astringent by the procedure used for bay oil.

In bay oil (1.40), others make unripe-olive oil astringent with cyperus, schoinos and calamus before boiling the bay in it.

Terebinth oil. Terebinth oil is prepared in the same way.

Uses

Cures mange in livestock and dogs; mixed into pessaries, remedies for fatigue and for leprous conditions; checks sweating. Terebinth oil cools and astringes.

The text

The approved English of the passage; turn on Greek (top right) to read Wellmann's Greek beside it.

Greek · Wellmann

τὸ δὲ σχίνινον ὁμοίως ἐκ τοῦ καρποῦ τῆς σχίνου πεπείρου σκευασθέν ἤ ὡς τὸ δάφνινον προστυφθὲν ὑγιάζει ψώρας κτηνῶν καὶ κυνῶν, καὶ εἰς πεσσοὺς καὶ εἰς ἄκοπα καὶ λεπρικὰ ἐπιτηδείως μείγνυται· στέλλει δὲ καὶ ἱδρῶτας. καὶ τὸ τερεβίνθινον δὲ ὁμοίως σκευάζεται· ψύχει δὲ καὶ στύφει.

English · approved translation

Mastic oil is obtained in the same fashion from the tree’s ripe fruit, or else rendered astringent by the procedure used for bay oil; it cures livestock and dogs of mange. It is also advantageously mixed into pessaries, remedies for fatigue, and remedies for leprous conditions, and it checks sweating. Terebinth oil is prepared in the same way; it cools and astringes.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.41, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

"In the same fashion" (ὁμοίως) refers back to bay oil (1.40), made from the ripe berries boiled with water.

No oil or amounts are named.

The chapter ends with terebinth oil, prepared in the same way (it cools and astringes); it is not a separate recipe in perfume-tables and is kept here.

The second plain step ("or else make it astringent by the procedure used for bay oil") is an alternative and is moved to Other ways.
