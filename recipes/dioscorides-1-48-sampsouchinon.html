σαμψουχῖνον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.48

Marjoram oil

draft

Six herbs (wild thyme, cassia, southernwood, water-mint flowers, myrtle leaves and marjoram) are pounded together and steeped in oil of unripe olives for four days, then pressed out. The same herbs, fresh, are steeped again for the same time, which makes the oil more potent. No weights are given: each herb is taken by estimate.

Method

  1. 1

    Pound the herbs. Pound the wild thyme, cassia, southernwood, water-mint flowers, myrtle leaves and marjoram together in one vessel.

    How much of each

    The text gives no weights: take each herb by estimate, with an eye to its strength.

  2. 2

    Add the oil. Pour on oil of unripe olives, only so much that it does not overpower the strength of the herbs steeped in it.

  3. 3

    Steep and press. Leave it for four days, then press it out.

  4. 4

    A second steeping. Steep the same amount of the same herbs, fresh, for the same time, and press them out. The oil is more potent made this way.

    In which oil

    The text does not say; presumably the oil from the first pressing, which is why it becomes more potent.

What to aim for

  • Choose oil that is dark olive in colour, smells strongly of marjoram and is moderately pungent.
  • A second steeping with fresh herbs makes it more potent.

Uses

Warming, attenuating and pungent: for closure and displacement of the uterus; it draws down the menses and afterbirth, revives women from uterine suffocation, and soothes pains of the lower back and groin; better used with honey, as it astringes so strongly that it hardens the parts; rubbed on, for fatigue; in poultices for opisthotonus and other spasms.

English · approved translation

Take wild thyme, cassia, southernwood, water-mint flowers, myrtle leaves, and marjoram, choosing the amount of each by estimation with due regard for its capacity. Pound everything together in the same vessel and pour on enough oil of unripe olives that the oil does not overpower the capacity of the ingredients steeped in it. Leave it for four days and press it out. Then steep the same quantity of the same ingredients, now fresh, for the same length of time, and press them out; the oil is more potent when prepared in this way. Choose oil that is dark olive in color, smells strongly of marjoram, and is moderately pungent.

2. It has a warming, attenuating, and pungent capacity. It is effective for closure and displacement of the uterus, draws down the menses and afterbirth, revives women suffering from uterine suffocation, and soothes pains of the lower back and groin. It is better to use it with honey, since it astringes the parts so strongly that it hardens them. When rubbed on, it also relieves fatigue. It is suitably mixed into poultices for those with opisthotonus and for others suffering from spasms.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.48, ed. Wellmann.
