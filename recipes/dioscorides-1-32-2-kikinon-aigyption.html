Egyptian castor oil

draft · Castor oil

The Egyptians, who need castor oil in abundance, make it another way. The cleaned seeds are ground in a mill, and the meal is put into baskets and pressed out.

Ingredients

Amount · metric

Castor seeds

cleaned, then milled; ripe when they come free from the capsules enclosing them

no amount given

Method

  1. 1

    Clean the seeds. Clean the castor seeds.

    Ripe seeds

    The text adds that castor seeds are ripe when they come free from the capsules enclosing them.

  2. 2

    Mill. Take them to a mill and grind them thoroughly.

  3. 3

    Press. Put the ground seeds into baskets and press them out with an implement.

    An implement

    The Greek says only ὄργανον, a device; what kind of press is not said.

Uses

For scabies, scalp eruptions, inflammations around the anus, closure and displacement of the uterus, disfiguring scars and earache; it makes plasters more effective; drunk, it purges watery matter and expels intestinal worms.

Greek · Wellmann

οἱ δὲ Αἰγύπτιοι διὰ τὸ δαψιλοῦς 2 χρῄζειν ἑτέρως αὐτὸ σκευάζουσι· μετὰ γὰρ τὸ καθᾶραι εἰς μύλην ἀποδόντες τοὺς κρότωνας ἀλήθουσιν ἐπιμελῶς, εἰς τὰς σφυρίδας δὲ ἐμβαλόντες τὸ ἀληλεσμένον δι᾿ ὀργάνου ἐκθλίβουσιν. ὥριμοι δέ εἰσιν οἱ κρότωνες, ὅταν ὑπὸ τῶν περιεχόντων αὐτοὺς σφαιρίων ἀπολύωνται. ποιεῖ δὲ τὸ κίκινον πρὸς ψώρας καὶ ἀχῶρας καὶ τὰς ἐν ἕδρᾳ φλεγμονάς, πρός τε ὑστέρας μύσεις καὶ διαστροφάς, ἔτι δὲ οὐλὰς ἀπρεπεῖς καὶ ὠταλγίας, ἐμπλάστροις τε μιγὲν ἐμπρακτοτέρας αὐτὰς ἐργάζεται. ἄγει δὲ καὶ κατὰ κοιλίαν ὑδατῶδες ποθέν, ἐκκρίνει δὲ καὶ ἕλμινθας.

English · approved translation

Because the Egyptians require it in abundance, they prepare it differently. After cleaning the castor seeds, they deliver them to a mill and grind them thoroughly. They then put the ground material into baskets and press it out with an implement. Castor seeds are ripe when they come free from the capsules enclosing them.

Castor oil is effective for scabies, scalp eruptions, inflammations around the anus, closure and displacement of the uterus, disfiguring scars, and earache. When mixed into plasters, it makes them more effective. When drunk, it also carries watery matter down through the bowels and expels intestinal worms.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.32.2, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The Egyptians' method, used because they need the oil in abundance. The cut begins at οἱ δὲ Αἰγύπτιοι; Wellmann prints the section number 2 inside that sentence (διὰ τὸ δαψιλοῦς 2 χρῄζειν).

The uses of castor oil (scabies, scalp eruptions, uterine complaints, scars, earache, in plasters, as a purge and against worms) stand in section 2 and stay with this recipe.
