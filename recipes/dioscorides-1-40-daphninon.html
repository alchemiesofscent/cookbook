δάφνινον daphninon

Bay oil

Bay oil family8 ingredients

Bay oil is made from bay berries ripe on the tree, boiled in water. The oily substance released from their skins is broken up by hand and gathered into a mussel shell. Dioscorides also describes bay leaves, and sometimes berries, boiled in unripe-olive oil made astringent with cyperus, schoinos and calamus.

Method

  1. Boil the berries Boil bay berries, ripe on the tree, in water.

  2. Gather the oil Break up by hand the oily substance the skins release and gather it into a mussel shell.

Other ways

Boiled in prepared oil Others make unripe-olive oil astringent with cyperus, schoinos and calamus, then add bay leaves and boil them together. Some add bay berries as well, until the oil smells strong enough. Some also mix in storax and myrtle.

Strong enough: the Greek prints κανὸν, presumably for ἱκανόν, “sufficiently”.

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides prefers mountain bay and broad-leaved bay for making it.
  • According to Dioscorides the fresh oil is superior: green, extremely bitter and pungent.
