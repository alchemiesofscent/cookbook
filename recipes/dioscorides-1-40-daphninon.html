δάφνινον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.40

Bay oil

draft · Bay oil

Bay berries ripe on the tree are boiled in water. The oily substance their skins release is broken up by hand and gathered into a mussel shell. Others boil bay leaves, and some berries as well, in unripe-olive oil made astringent with aromatics.

Method

  1. 1

    Boil the berries. Boil bay berries that have ripened on the tree in water.

  2. 2

    Gather the oil. The skins release an oily substance. Break it up by hand and gather it into a mussel shell.

Other ways

Boiled in prepared oil. Others make unripe-olive oil astringent with cyperus, schoinos and calamus, then add bay leaves and boil them together. Some add bay berries as well, until the oil smells strong enough. Some also mix in storax and myrtle.

Strong enough: the Greek prints κανὸν, presumably for ἱκανόν, “sufficiently”.

What to aim for

  • Mountain bay and broad-leaved bay are best for making it.
  • Fresh bay oil is best: green, extremely bitter and pungent.

Uses

Warming, softening, opening and relieving fatigue: for all disorders of the sinews, shivering fits, earache, catarrhs and headache; drunk, it causes nausea.

Greek · Wellmann

τὸ δὲ δάφνινον σκευάζεται διὰ τῶν δαφνίδων, ὅταν ὦσι δρυπεπεῖς, ἑψομένων μεθ᾿ ὕδατος· ἐκ γὰρ τοῦ περικειμένου λέπους ἀνίησι λιπαρίαν τινά, ἣν διακόψαντες χερσὶν εἰς μύακα ἀναλαμβάνουσιν, ἔνιοι δὲ ὀμφάκινον ἔλαιον προστύψαντες κυπέρῳ καὶ σχοίνῳ καὶ καλάμῳ καὶ μετὰ ταῦτα δάφνης φύλλα περιβαλόντες συνέψουσιν, οἱ δὲ καὶ δαφνίδας, ἕως ἂν κανὸν γένηται τῇ ὀσμῇ ἔνιοι δὲ καὶ στύρακα μίσγουσι καὶ μυρσίνην. ἀρίστη δὲ πρὸς σκευασίαν ἡ ὀρεινὴ καὶ πλατύφυυλλος δάφνη· 2 διαφέρει δὲ τοῦ δαφνίνου τὸ πρόσφατον, χλωρὸν τῇ χρόᾳ, πικρότατον, δριμύ. δύναμιν δὲ ἔχει θερμαντικήν, μαλακτικήν, ἀναστομωτικήν, κόπων λυτικήν· ἁρμόζει καὶ τοῖς περὶ τὰ νεῦρα πάθεσι πᾶσι καὶ φρίκαις καὶ ὠταλγίαις καὶ κατάρροις καὶ κεφαλαλγίαις. ἔστι δὲ καὶ ναυτιῶδες ποθέν.

English · approved translation

Bay oil is prepared from bay berries once they are ripe on the tree, by boiling them with water. The skin surrounding them releases an oily substance; after breaking it up with their hands, they gather it into a mussel shell. Others make omphacine oil astringent with cyperus, rush, and calamus, then add bay leaves and boil the mixture together. Others add bay berries as well, until the oil becomes sufficiently fragrant, while some also mix in storax and myrtle. Mountain bay and broad-leaved bay are best for the preparation.

Fresh bay oil is superior: it is green in color, extremely bitter, and pungent. It has a warming, softening, opening, and fatigue-relieving capacity. It is suitable for every disorder affecting the sinews, as well as for shivering fits, earache, catarrhs, and headache. When drunk, however, it also causes nausea.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.40, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The oily substance (λιπαρίαν τινά) the berries release is the product itself, not an ingredient; it is not listed.

Others add bay berries as well, until the oil is fragrant enough; some also mix in storax and myrtle.

Mountain bay and broad-leaved bay are best for the preparation. Fresh bay oil is superior: green, extremely bitter and pungent.

The Greek prints κανὸν (presumably for ἱκανὸν, 'sufficiently'), has no stop before ἔνιοι δὲ καὶ στύρακα, and prints πλατύφυυλλος: all copied as they stand.

Wellmann's section number 2 falls at the start of the passage on quality and uses (δάφνη· 2 διαφέρει); the whole chapter is one recipe, so nothing is cut.

The water for boiling the berries is moved to the working materials; the bay berries of the others' method (δαφνίδας) are folded into the one bay-berry ingredient.
