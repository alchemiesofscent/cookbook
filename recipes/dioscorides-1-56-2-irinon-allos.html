Recipe

ἴρινον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.56.2

Iris unguent, a second way

draft · Iris unguent

g of prepared oil the written batch

Another way to make iris unguent. Oil is boiled with chopped balsam wood, which is then taken out, and chopped calamus and a lump of myrrh moistened with wine are added. An equal weight of chopped iris is steeped in 14 pounds of this oil for two days and two nights and pressed out hard; for a stronger unguent the steeping is repeated two or three times.

Method

  1. 1

    Boil the balsam wood. Chop 5 lb 2 oz of balsam wood, as stated, and boil it with 9 lb 5 oz of oil.

    As stated

    Probably as in the first method of the chapter: chopped as finely as possible.

  2. 2

    Add the calamus and myrrh. Take out the balsam wood and add 9 lb 10 oz of chopped calamus, with a lump of myrrh moistened with fragrant aged wine.

  3. 3

    Steep the iris. Measure out 14 lb of this prepared oil and steep in it the same weight of chopped iris. Leave it for two days and two nights.

  4. 4

    Press. Press it out forcefully and vigorously.

  5. 5

    A stronger unguent. For a stronger unguent, steep the same weight of iris in it two or three times more in the same way, pressing it out after each steeping.

What to aim for

  • Each further steeping with the same weight of iris makes it more potent.

English · approved translation

2. Others proceed as follows. Chop five litrai and two oungiai of balsam wood as stated and boil it together with nine litrai and five oungiai of oil. Then remove the balsam wood and add nine litrai and ten oungiai of chopped aromatic reed, together with a lump of myrrh moistened with fragrant aged wine. Then measure out fourteen litrai of the aromatized astringent oil and soak the same weight of chopped iris in it. Leave it for two days and two nights, then press it out forcefully and vigorously. If you want it to be more potent, repeat the steeping in the same way with the same weight of iris two or three times, pressing it out after each steeping.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.56.2, ed. Wellmann.
