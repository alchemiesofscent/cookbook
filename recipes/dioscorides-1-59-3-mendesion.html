Mendesian unguent

draft · Mendesian unguent

The Mendesian unguent is made from balanos oil, myrrh, cassia and resin. No amounts or method are given. It acts like metopion, but more weakly.

Method

  1. 1

    Compound it. Make it from balanos oil, myrrh, cassia and resin.

    Boiled together

    The text implies the ingredients are boiled together: what is not boiled with the rest does not give up its strength.

Other ways

Cinnamon after the weighing. Some add a little cinnamon after the ingredients have been weighed, but to no benefit: ingredients not boiled together do not release their strength.

Uses

Like metopion, but weaker and less intense.

The text

The approved English of the passage; turn on Greek (top right) to read Wellmann's Greek beside it.

Greek · Wellmann

καὶ τὸ Μενδήσιον δὲ λεγόμενον ἔκ τε βαλανίνου ἐλαίου 3 καὶ σμύρνης καὶ κασσίας καὶ ῥητίνης σκευάζεται. ἔνιοι δὲ μετὰ τὸν κατασταθμισμὸν κινάμωμον μικρὸν προσεμβάλλουσιν ἀνωφελῶς· οὐκ ἐκδίδωσι γὰρ τὰ μὴ συνεψηγμένα τὴν δύναμιν. ἐμφερῆ δὲ ἔχει τὴν ἐνέργειαν τῷ μετωπίῳ, ἥττονα μέντοι καὶ οὐχ οὕτως ἐπιτεταμένην.

English · approved translation

The preparation called Mendesian unguent is made from ben oil, myrrh, cassia, and resin. After the ingredients have been weighed, some people add a little cinnamon, but without benefit, since ingredients that have not been boiled together do not release their capacity. Its activity resembles that of metopion, though it is weaker and not so intense.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.59.3, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

Wellmann's section number 3 is misplaced in the Greek (βαλανίνου ἐλαίου 3 καὶ σμύρνης): section 3 really begins at καὶ τὸ Μενδήσιον, where the cut is made. perfume-tables read the 3 as a quantity of oil; dropped.

Its activity resembles that of metopion (dioscorides-1-59-metopion), though weaker and less intense. No amounts are given.
