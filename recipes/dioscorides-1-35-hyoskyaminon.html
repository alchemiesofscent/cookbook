ὑοσκυάμινον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.35

Henbane oil

draft

Henbane oil is made from the white seed of henbane, pounded and kneaded with hot water as for almond oil. The mass is worked in the sun until it turns black and smells foul, then pressed through a woven strainer and stored.

TimeSun until it turns black and foul-smellingNo days or temperatures are given: colour and smell tell you when to stop.

Method

  1. 1

    Pound and knead. Take the white seed, dry and fresh, and pound it. Knead it with hot water, as for almond oil.

    As for almond oil

    In almond oil (1.33) the pounded paste soaks up hot water and is then worked again. No amounts are given here.

  2. 2

    Work it in the sun. Set the portions that dry as you work in the sun and mix them back into the whole. Keep doing this until it turns black and smells foul.

    What is sunned

    The Greek can also be read: keep the mass in the sun, and mix back into it the parts that dry under your hands.

  3. 3

    Press and store. Press it out through a woven strainer and store it.

    Woven strainer

    φορμός, a plaited basket or mat.

What to aim for

  • Keep working it until it turns black and smells foul.

Uses

For earache; emollient, so it is mixed into pessaries.

The text

The approved English of the passage; turn on Greek (top right) to read Wellmann's Greek beside it.

Greek · Wellmann

καὶ τὸ ὑ ὑοσκυάμινον δὲ σκευάζεται οὕτως· λαβὼν τὸν λευκὸν καρπὸν ξηρόν, πρόσφατον καὶ κόψας ὕδατι θερμῷ φύρα, ὡς προείρηται ἐπὶ τοῦ ἀμυγδαλίνου, ἡλιάζων τε τὰ ὑπὸ χεῖρα ξηραινόμενα αὐτοῦ μέρη τῷ ὅλῳ ἀνάμισγε καὶ τὸ αὐτὸ ποίει, ἄχρις ὅτου ἂν μελανθῇ καὶ δυσῶδες γένηται, εἶτα διὰ φορμοῦ ἐκθλίψας ἀποτίθεσο. ποιεῖ δὲ καὶ πρὸς ὠταλγίας καὶ πεσσοῖς μείγνυται μαλακτικὸν ὄν.

English · approved translation

Henbane oil is prepared as follows. Take the dry, fresh white seed, pound it, and knead it with hot water, as stated above for almond oil. Set the portions that are drying as you work in the sun, mix them back into the whole, and keep repeating the process until it turns black and becomes foul-smelling. Then press it through a woven strainer and store it. It is also effective for earache and, because it is emollient, is mixed into pessaries.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.35, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The Greek opens καὶ τὸ ὑ ὑοσκυάμινον: the stray ὑ is copied as it stands.

The method refers to almond oil (1.33) for the kneading.

The five plain steps are grouped as three: pounding with kneading, and pressing with storing.
