ἰασμέλαιον iasmelaion

Iasmelaion

2 ingredients

ml of σησαμίνου ἐλαίου (sesame oil) as written

Iasmelaion is sesame oil steeped with the white flowers of the violet, 2 ounces to an Italian xestes. The flowers are replaced with fresh ones in turn, as for lily oil (1.52). According to Dioscorides it is called iasme and made by the Persians, who use it at banquets for its fragrance, though many find its heavy scent unpleasant.

Ingredients

  • ἰοῦ iou

    Its white flowers.

    2 ounces

    54–55 g

    οὐγγίων βʹ

    9.8% of the batch

  • σησαμίνου ἐλαίου sesaminou elaiou

    An Italian xestes (sextarius).

    1 xestes

    547 ml

    Ἰταλικῷ ξέστῃ

    90.2% of the batch

Metric figures are ranges, from the pound (λίτρα) of 322–329 g and the other units; the scaler changes them and leaves the ancient amounts as written.

Method

  1. Steep the flowers Steep 2 ounces (54–55 g) of the white flowers of the violet in 1 Italian xestes (547 ml) of sesame oil.

  2. Change the flowers Replace the flowers with fresh ones in turn, as for lily oil (1.52).

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides calls its scent heavy, so that many do not find it pleasant.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.63 (apparatus), ed. M. Wellmann, Berlin 1907, vol. 1, p. 57.

Translation: Alchemies of Scent, provisional.
