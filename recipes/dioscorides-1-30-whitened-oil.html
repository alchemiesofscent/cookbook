λευκανθὲν ἔλαιον leukanthen elaion

Whitened oil

6 ingredientsTime · sun, rest, again

ml of ἔλαιον (oil) as written

Whitened oil is pale olive oil, no more than a year old, bleached in the sun in a wide earthenware vessel. It is beaten with a ladle every day until it foams, and on the eighth day soaked fenugreek and split resinous pine go in for eight days more. The oil is drawn off and stored over dried melilot and iris, or, if it is not yet white, sunned and worked again.

Metric figures are ranges, from the pound (λίτρα) of 322–329 g and the other units; the scaler changes them and leaves the ancient amounts as written.

Method

  1. Set the oil in the sun Pour 100 kotylai (27.4 l) of pale oil, no more than a year old, into a new, wide-mouthed earthenware vessel and set it in the sun.

  2. Beat it every day Lift the oil from the centre with a ladle every day and pour it back from high above, so that the constant beating makes it change and foam.

    Eight days The fenugreek goes in on the eighth day.

  3. Add fenugreek and pine Soak 50 drachmai (168–172 g) of clean fenugreek in warm water on the eighth day. When it is soft, add it to the oil with its water, along with an equal weight of a firebrand of the most resinous pine, split into fine pieces.

    An equal weight 50 drachmas, the weight of the fenugreek.

  4. Leave eight more days Leave it as it is for 8 more days.

  5. Draw off and store Draw off the oil with the ladle. If it has come out as it should, rinse a new vessel with aged wine, lay 11 holkai (37–38 g) of dried melilot garlands and as much iris in the bottom, pour in the oil and store it.

  6. If it is not yet white Set it in the sun again if it is not yet white, and keep working it until it is.

What to aim for

  • Pale oil, no more than a year old, to start.
  • Daily beating until the oil changes and foams.
  • A white oil at the end; if it is not white, sun it and work it again.
