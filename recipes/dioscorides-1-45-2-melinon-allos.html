Quince oil, a second way

draft · Quince oil

Others wrap the quinces in cloth for ten days to keep in their fragrance. Then they soak them in the oil for two days and nights, press them out and store the oil.

Method

  1. 1

    Wrap the quinces. Wrap the quinces in cloth for ten days, so that the fragrance is held in and does not escape.

  2. 2

    Soak in the oil. Soak the fruit in the oil for two days and nights.

  3. 3

    Press and store. Press it out and put the oil away.

Uses

Astringent and cooling: for scabby ulcers, dandruff, chilblains and creeping eruptions; by instillation for ulcers of the uterus; as an enema, it restrains irritation of the urinary passages; it checks sweating; drunk against cantharides, buprestes and the pine processionary caterpillar.

English · approved translation

2. Others envelop the quinces in cloth for ten days to confine the fragrance and keep it from dissipating; they then soak the fruit in the oil for two days and nights, press it out, and put the oil away. It has an astringent and cooling capacity. It is effective for scabby ulcers, dandruff, chilblains, and creeping eruptions, and also for ulcerations in the uterus when introduced by instillation. When administered as an enema, it restrains irritation of the urinary passages, and it also checks sweating. It is also drunk against cantharides, buprestes, and the pine processionary caterpillar. The approved kind is the one that smells of quinces.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.45.2, ed. Wellmann.
