στακτή · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.60

Stacte

draft

Stacte is the oily part of fresh myrrh. The myrrh is pounded with a little water and pressed through an instrument; what runs off is, by itself, the perfume called stacte.

Ingredients

Amount · metric

Myrrh

σμύρνης · σμύρνα

Fresh myrrh, pounded; its oily part is the stacte.

no amount given

Working materials

Water for pounding the myrrh

ὕδατος · ὕδωρ

A little; no measure given.

as printed

ὀλίγου

Method

  1. 1

    Pound the myrrh. Pound fresh myrrh with a little water.

  2. 2

    Press out the stacte. Press it through an instrument. The oily part that comes away is the stacte.

    An instrument

    The text does not describe it; it says only that the myrrh is pressed "through an instrument".

What to aim for

  • It is very fragrant and costly.
  • The best is unmixed with oil, and a very small amount of it has very great strength.

Uses

Warming, like myrrh and the warming unguents.

The text

The approved English of the passage; turn on Greek (top right) to read Wellmann's Greek beside it.

Greek · Wellmann

στακτὴ δὲ καλεῖται τῆς προσφάτου σμύρνης τὸ λιπαρόν, κεκομμένης μεθʼ ὕδατος ὀλίγου ἀποτεθλιμμένης τε διʼ ὀργάνου. εὐώδης δὲ λίαν καὶ πολυτελής ἐστι [καὶ] καθʼ ἑαυτὴν οὖσα μύρον τὸ καλούμενον στακτή. δόκιμος δέ ἐστιν ἡ ἀμιγὴς ἐλαίου καὶ ἐν ἐλαχίστῳ πλείστην δύναμιν κεκτημένη, οὖσαν θερμαντικήν, ἀναλογοῦσαν τῇ σμύρνῃ καὶ τοῖς θερμαίνουσι τῶν μύρων.

English · approved translation

Stacte is the oily part of fresh myrrh, obtained by pounding the myrrh with a little water and then pressing it through an instrument. It is exceedingly fragrant and costly [and], by itself, constitutes the unguent called stacte. A satisfactory specimen is unmixed with oil and possesses a very great capacity in a very small quantity. This capacity is warming and corresponds to that of myrrh and of warming unguents.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.60, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The whole chapter is one recipe; nothing is cut.

ἐλαίου (ἀμιγὴς ἐλαίου, 'unmixed with oil') is a test of quality, not an ingredient, and is not listed.

The Greek keeps Wellmann's bracketed [καὶ]; the English renders it [and].

The water is listed as a working material (for pounding the myrrh), as the water for the schoinos is on the rose oil.
