ὠκιμῖνον · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.49

Basil oil

draft

g of aromatised oil the written batch

Basil leaves are steeped for a night and a day in oil already aromatised as for henna oil, then pressed out; the same leaves take one more pouring of oil, giving a second-grade oil. For a stronger oil, fresh basil is steeped in the oil already used, as for rose oil, up to three or four times.

Method

  1. 1

    Steep the basil. Strip the leaves from 11 lb 8 oz (by weight) of basil and steep them in 20 lb of the aromatised oil for a night and a day.

    The oil

    Oil aromatised as for henna oil (kyprinon), described in 1.55.

  2. 2

    Press and store. Press out the oil and store it.

  3. 3

    The second-grade oil. Empty the leaves out of the straining basket, pour the same amount of oil over them again, and press it out. This is called the second-grade oil: the leaves will not take a third steeping.

    The straining basket

    The leaves are pressed out in a basket (κυρτίς); here they are emptied out of it to take more oil.

  4. 4

    A stronger oil. Take the same amount of fresh basil and steep it, as for rose oil, pouring over it the oil already steeped. Leave it for the same time, then press it out and store it.

    As for rose oil

    In rose oil (1.43) fresh roses are steeped in the oil already made, to strengthen it.

  5. 5

    Further steepings. If you wish, steep it three or four times, always with fresh basil.

Other ways

With unripe-olive oil. It can also be made from oil of unripe olives, but the first way is better.

What to aim for

  • The pressed leaves take a second pouring of oil, the second-grade oil, but not a third.
  • Made with the aromatised oil it is better than with oil of unripe olives.

Uses

It does the same as marjoram oil (1.48), but acts less strongly.

Greek · Wellmann

ὠκιμίνον σκευασία· τοῦ εἰς τὸ κύπρινον ἠρωματισμένου, ὡς ἀποδείξομεν (I 55), λαβὼν ἐλαίου λίτρας εἴκοσι, ὠκίμου σταθμῷ λίτρας ἕνδεκα οὐγγίας ὀκτὼ ἀποφυλλίσας ἀπόβρεχε νύκτα καὶ ἡμέραν ἐν τῷ ἐλαίῳ, εἶτα ἐξίπου καὶ ἀποτίθεσο. ἐκκενώσας δὲ τὸ ἀπὸ τῆς κυρτίδος ἐπίχει πάλιν τὸ ἴσον πλῆθος τοῦ ἐλαίου καὶ ἐξίπου. καλεῖται δὲ τοῦτο δευτερεῖον· τρίτην γὰρ οὐκ ἐπιδέχεται βροχήν. εἶτα προσφάτου ὠκίμου λαβὼν τὸ ἴσον πάλιν ἀπόβρεχε, ὡς ἐπὶ τοῦ ῥοδίνου εἴρηται (I 43), τὸ δὲ ἤδη ἀποβεβρεγμένον ἐπιχέων καὶ ἐῶν τὸν αὐτὸν χρόνον ἔκθλιβε καὶ ἀποτίθεσο. κἂν τρὶς δὲ ἢ τετράκις θέλῃς, ἀπόβρεχε πρόσφατον ἀεὶ ὤκιμον ἐμβάλλων. δύναται δὲ καὶ ἐκ τοῦ ὀμφοκίνου σκευάζεσθαι· βέλτιον δὲ ἐκείνως. δύναται δὲ τὸ αὐτὸ τῷ σαμψουχίνῳ, ἔλαττον ἐνεργοῦν.

English · approved translation

Take twenty litrai of the oil aromatized for making henna oil, as we shall explain below (I 55). Strip basil leaves amounting by weight to eleven litrai and eight ounces, and steep them in the oil for one night and one day; then press out the oil and store it. Empty the material from the straining basket, pour the same quantity of oil over it again, and press it out. This is called the second-grade oil, since the material will not admit a third steeping.

Next take the same quantity of fresh basil and steep it again, as stated for rose oil (I 43), pouring over it the oil already used for steeping. Leave it for the same length of time, then press it out and store it. If you wish, you may steep it three or four times, always adding fresh basil. It can also be prepared from oil of unripe olives, but the former method is better. It can accomplish the same things as marjoram oil, though it acts less strongly.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.49, ed. Wellmann.
