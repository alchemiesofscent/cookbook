ὠκιμῖνον okiminon

Basil oil

5 ingredientsTime · steep, steep fresh basil

g of ἐλαίου (Aromatised oil) as written

Basil oil is made from basil leaves steeped in oil already aromatised as for henna oil (1.55). The leaves steep for a night and a day and are pressed out, then take one more pouring of oil and are pressed again, and fresh basil can be steeped in the oil already used, as for rose oil (1.43), up to three or four times. Dioscorides allows oil of unripe olives as a base but prefers the aromatised oil.

    An alternative base; Dioscorides prefers the aromatised oil. Our Greek text reads ὀμφοκίνου; we read ὀμφακίνου, "of unripe-olive oil".

Metric figures are ranges, from the pound (λίτρα) of 322–329 g and the other units; the scaler changes them and leaves the ancient amounts as written.

Method

  1. Steep the basil Strip the leaves from 11 lb 8 oz (3.8 kg) (by weight) of basil and steep them in 20 lb (6.4–6.6 kg) of the aromatised oil for a night and a day.

    The oil Oil aromatised as for henna oil (kyprinon), described in 1.55.

  2. Press and store Press out the oil and store it.

  3. The second-grade oil Empty the leaves out of the straining basket, pour the same amount of oil over them again, and press it out; do not steep them a third time.

    The straining basket The leaves are pressed out in a basket (κυρτίς); here they are emptied out of it to take more oil.

  4. A stronger oil Take the same amount of fresh basil and steep it, as for rose oil, pouring over it the oil already steeped. Leave it for the same time, then press it out and store it.

    As for rose oil In rose oil (1.43) fresh roses are steeped in the oil already made, to strengthen it.

  5. Further steepings Steep it three or four times if you wish, always with fresh basil.

Other ways

With unripe-olive oil It can also be made from oil of unripe olives, but the first way is better.

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides calls the second pouring over the pressed leaves the second-grade oil, and says the leaves take no third.
  • Dioscorides prefers it made with the aromatised oil to oil of unripe olives.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.49, ed. M. Wellmann, Berlin 1907, vol. 1, p. 46.
