Ῥόδινον · Paul of Aegina, Epitome 7.20.4

Rose oil

draft · Rose oil

ml of unripe-olive oil the written batch

The simple rose oil: red rose petals, their bases removed and aired for a day and a night, are sealed in a jar with unripe-olive oil and left outdoors in the sun for forty days. Some keep the jar in a well or bury it instead.

TimeAir the petals a day and a nightSun forty days, outdoorsNo temperatures are given. In the well method the jar stays down for the same forty days.

Method

  1. 1

    Prepare the roses. Take three ounces of red rose petals. Remove their nail-like bases and leave them to air for a day and a night.

    Nail-like bases

    The Greek ὠνυχισμένων is the same word Aetius uses (ἐξωνυχισμένων), which his translation renders "calyces removed".

  2. 2

    Add the oil and seal. Put them in a jar with one Italian sextarius of unripe-olive oil. Seal the jar carefully so that its contents do not evaporate.

  3. 3

    Sun. Leave the jar outdoors in the sun for forty days.

    Forty days

    Aetius (1.113) gives twenty days in the sun for the same oil.

  4. 4

    Store. Then store it on a plank, not on the ground.

    Straining

    Paul does not mention straining; Aetius strains the oil before storing it.

Other ways

In a well. Instead of sunning the jar, some lower it into a well for the forty days, so that the cold preserves the fragrance.

In the earth. Others bury the jar in the earth.

How long is not stated.

Greek · Heiberg

Ῥόδων ἐρυθρῶν ὠνυχισμένων καὶ διεψυγμένων νυχθήμερον α 𐆄 γ, ἐλαίου ὀμφακίνου ξ̸ Ἰταλικὸν α· περισφίγξας ἐπιμελῶς τὸν βῖκον, ὡς μὴ διαπνεῖσθαι, ἡλίου ἐν ὑπαίθρῳ ἐπὶ μ ἡμέρας καὶ τότε ἀποτίθεσο μὴ ἐπ' ἐδάφους ἀλλ' ἐπὶ σανίδος. τινὲς δὲ ἀντὶ τῆς ἡλιώσεως καθίασιν εἰς φρέαρ τὸν βῖκον τὰς μ ἡμέρας διὰ τὸ ἐκ τῆς ψύξεως διαφυλαχθῆναι τὴν εὐωδίαν, ἄλλοι δὲ κατορύττουσιν αὐτὸν ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς.

English · approved translation

Take three ounces of red rose petals whose nail-like bases have been removed and which have been aired for a day and a night, and one Italian sextarius of unripe-olive oil. Seal the jar carefully so that its contents do not evaporate, and leave it outdoors in the sun for forty days. Then store it on a plank rather than on the ground. Instead of sunning the jar, some lower it into a well for the forty days, so that the cold preserves the fragrance; others bury it in the earth.

Source

Paul of Aegina, Epitome 7.20.4, ed. Heiberg.

Editor's notes

Greek: Heiberg (CMG IX.2) as given in the oribasius-app pass-a packet for PAUL_EPIT-7.20-s04; English: the accepted pass-a result for the same entry.

The α after νυχθήμερον is read as 'one' (a single day and night), not as part of the amount; the amount is 𐆄 γ, three ounces. μ is the numeral forty.

The Italian sextarius (ξ̸ Ἰταλικόν) is taken to be the xestes of units.json.

The simple rose oil, roses and unripe-olive oil only; Aetius 1.113 gives the same proportion (three ounces of roses to an Italian sextarius) but twenty days in the sun instead of forty.

Plain step 5 (the well or burial instead of sunning) is moved to other_ways; plain steps 1 and 2 become "Prepare the roses" and "Add the oil and seal".
