Paul of Aegina · Epitome 7.20.4 · Heiberg

Ῥόδινον Rhodinon

Rose oil

Rose oil family2 ingredientsTime · air the petals, sun

ml of ἐλαίου ὀμφακίνου (Unripe-olive oil) as written

Rose oil is made from red rose petals and unripe-olive oil, 3 oz to an Italian sextarius. The petals, their nail-like bases removed and aired for a day and a night, are sealed in a jar with the oil and left outdoors in the sun for forty days. Paul says some lower the jar into a well instead, so that the cold keeps the fragrance, and others bury it.

Metric figures are ranges, from the pound (λίτρα) of 322–329 g and the other units; the scaler changes them and leaves the ancient amounts as written.

Method

  1. Prepare the roses Take 3 oz (80–82 g) of red rose petals. Remove their nail-like bases and leave them to air for a day and a night.

    Nail-like bases The same step as in Aetius' rose oil (1.113), where the calyces are removed.

  2. Add the oil and seal Put them in a jar with 1 Italian xestes (547 ml) of unripe-olive oil. Seal the jar carefully so that nothing evaporates.

  3. Sun Leave the jar outdoors in the sun for forty days.

    Forty days Aetius' rose oil (1.113) stays twenty days in the sun.

  4. Store Store it on a plank, not on the ground.

    Straining Aetius strains his rose oil before storing it (1.113).

Other ways

In a well Instead of sunning the jar, some lower it into a well for the forty days; Paul gives their reason, that the cold preserves the fragrance.

In the earth Others bury the jar in the earth.

How long is not stated.
