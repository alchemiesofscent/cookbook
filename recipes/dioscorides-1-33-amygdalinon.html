Almond oil

draft · Almond oil

ml of bitter almonds the written batch

Almond oil, which some call metopion, is pressed from bitter almonds. The almonds are crushed to a paste, soaked with hot water, ground again and pressed out against a board; water is added to the pressed mass and it is pressed a second time. Four choinikes of almonds give one kotyle of oil.

TimeSoak half an hour, with the first waterThe second soaking is not timed, and no temperatures are given beyond hot water.

Method

  1. 1

    Crush the almonds. Clean and dry four choinikes of bitter almonds. Crush them gently in a mortar with a wooden pestle until they become a paste.

  2. 2

    Soak and grind again. Pour two kotylai of hot water over the paste and let it soak in for half an hour. Then grind it again, harder this time.

  3. 3

    Press out. Press the paste out against a board, a little at a time, and collect what sticks to your fingers in a spoon.

    Spoon

    The Greek word, μύαξ, is a mussel shell, used here as a spoon.

  4. 4

    Press a second time. Pour one kotyle of water onto the pressed mass, let it soak in, and press it out the same way.

    Yield

    Four choinikes of almonds give one kotyle of oil.

Uses

For pains, suffocation, displacement and inflammation of the uterus; for headache, earache, and noises and whistling in the ears; for kidney disorders, difficult urination, stones, asthma and the spleen; with honey, lily root and kypros or rose cerate it clears marks, sunspots and wrinkles from the face; for dim sight; with wine it cleans away scalp eruptions and dandruff.

Greek · Wellmann

ἀμυγδάλινον ἔλαιον, ὅ τινες μετώπιον καλοῦσι, σκευάζεται οὕτως· ἀμυγδάλων πικρῶν χοίνικας τέσσαρας καθάρας καὶ ξηράνας κόψον ὑπέρῳ ξυλίνῳ ἐν ὅλμῳ κούφως, μέχρι μᾶζα γένηται, ἐπιχέας τε ζεστοῦ ὕδατος κοτύλας δύο ἔασον συμπιεῖν ἡμιώριον, ἀφ᾿ ἧς πάλιν βιαιότερον τρῖβε. εἶτα ἀποθλίβων πρὸς πλάκα ἐξίπου καὶ τὸ ἀπὸ τῶν δακτύλων εἰς μύακα ἀναλάμβανε, εἶτα πάλιν εἰς τὸ ἀποτεθλιμμένον κοτύλην μίαν ὕδατος ἐπιχέας καὶ συμπιεῖν ἐάσας τὸ αὐτὸ ποίει· ἀποδίδωσι δὲ κοτύλην μίαν τὸ τετραχοίνικον. ποιεῖ δὲ πρὸς τὰς ἐν μήτρᾳ ἀλγηδόνας καὶ πνίγας ὑστερικὰς 2 διαστροφάς τε καὶ φλεγμονὰς τῶν αὐτῶν τόπων, ἔτι δὲ κεφαλαλγίας καὶ ὤτων πόνους καὶ ἤχους καὶ συριγμούς· ὠφελεῖ δὲ καὶ νεφριτικοὺς καὶ δυσουριῶντας, λιθιῶντας, ἀσθματικοὺς καὶ σπληνικοὺς. αἴρει δὲ καὶ σπίλους ἐκ προσώπου καὶ ἐφήλεις καὶ ῥυτίδας μέλιτι μειγνύμενον καὶ κρίνου ῥίζῃ καὶ κυπρίνῃ ἢ ῥοδίνῃ κηρωτῇ, καὶ ἀμβλυωπίας δέ ἐστιν ἀνασκευαστικόν. σὺν οἴνῳ δὲ ἀχῶρας καὶ πίτυρα σμήχει.

English · approved translation

The oil of almonds—also known to some as metopion—is made in the following manner. Take four choinikes of bitter almonds, clean and dry them, and gently crush them in a mortar with a wooden pestle until they become a paste. Add two kotylai of hot water, allow the paste to absorb it for half an hour, and then grind it again, this time more vigorously. Next, press it out against a board little by little, collecting what adheres to your fingers into a spoon. Then pour one kotyle of water onto the pressed mass, allow it to be absorbed, and repeat the same process. Four choinikes yield one kotyle.

It is effective for pains in the uterus, uterine suffocation, displacement and inflammation of the same parts, as well as headache, ear pain, noises in the ears, and whistling sounds. It also benefits those with kidney disorders, difficulty urinating, stones, asthma, and disorders of the spleen. When mixed with honey, lily root, and either kypros cerate or rose cerate, it removes marks, sunspots, and wrinkles from the face; it is also remedial for dimness of sight. With wine, it cleans away scalp eruptions and dandruff.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.33, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

Four choinikes yield one kotyle (ἀποδίδωσι δὲ κοτύλην μίαν τὸ τετραχοίνικον).

Wellmann prints the section number 2 inside a sentence (πνίγας ὑστερικὰς 2 διαστροφάς); the whole chapter is one recipe, so nothing is cut.

The six plain steps are grouped as four: cleaning with crushing, and soaking with the second grinding. Both waters stay among the ingredients, each with its own amount: they are pressed out with the oil, not thrown away.
