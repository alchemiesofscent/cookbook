Radish oil

draft · Radish oil

Radish oil is made from radish seed in the same way as the other oils. The text gives no method or amounts of its own.

Ingredients

Amount · metric

Radish seed

no amount given

Method

  1. 1

    Make the oil. Make the oil from radish seed in the same way as the other oils.

    The other oils

    The oils just before (1.33–1.36) are pounded, worked with hot water and pressed.

Uses

For lice that come with illness; scours rough patches on the face. In Egypt it is cooked with side dishes.

The text

The approved English of the passage; turn on Greek (top right) to read Wellmann's Greek beside it.

Greek · Wellmann

τὸ δὲ ῥαφάνινον σκευάζεται ἐκ τοῦ αὐτῆς σπέρματος ὁμοίως ὥσπερ καὶ τὰ λοιπά. ἁρμόζει δὲ τοῖς ἐκ νόσου φθειριῶσι καὶ τὰς περὶ τὸ πρόσωπον σμήχει τραχύτητας. χρῶνται δὲ αὐτῷ οἱ ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ τοῖς προσοψήμασι συνέψοντες αὐτό.

English · approved translation

Radish oil is prepared from its seed in the same way as the other oils. It is suitable for those who become infested with lice as a result of illness, and it scours away rough patches around the face. The people of Egypt use it by cooking it together with their side dishes.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.37 (Wellmann 39.5–10), ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

Chapter 1.37 holds two entries. The Greek and English stop before καὶ τὸ μελάνθινον ("Black-seed oil has …"), ported as the report dioscorides-1-37-melanthinon. Wellmann prints no section number inside the chapter; the locator gives perfume-tables' Wellmann page and lines.

The Egyptians cook the oil with their side dishes (τοῖς προσοψήμασι): a use, not an ingredient.
