ῥητίνη κεκαυμένη μετὰ ὕδατος · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.71.5

Resin processed with water

draft

ml of resin the written batch

Liquid resin, first strained while melted, is boiled gently with twice its measure of rainwater in a bronze vessel over coals, stirring all the time, until it is odourless, crumbly and dry. It is then cooled and stored in an unpitched earthenware vessel; it comes out very white.

Method

  1. 1

    Strain the resin. First melt the resin and strain it, so that its dirt is separated out.

    First

    The English gives this at the end ("must first be strained"); it is done before the boiling.

  2. 2

    Boil with rainwater. Put one chous of resin and two choes of rainwater into a bronze vessel. Boil gently over coals, stirring continuously.

    The vessel

    It should hold four times the volume of the liquid poured into it; the text does not say whether the water is counted.

  3. 3

    Until dry and crumbly. Keep boiling until it has no smell, crumbles easily, and is so dry that it no longer yields to the fingers.

  4. 4

    Cool and store. Let it cool, then store it in an untreated, that is unpitched, earthenware vessel.

What to aim for

  • It is done when it has no smell, crumbles easily, and no longer yields to the fingers.
  • It becomes exceptionally white.

English · approved translation

5. Every liquid resin is processed by heating in a vessel four times the volume of the liquid poured into it. Put one chous of resin and two choes of rainwater into a bronze vessel and boil gently over coals, stirring continuously, until it becomes odorless, readily crumbled, and so thoroughly dry that it no longer yields to the fingers. Then let it cool and store it in an untreated earthenware vessel [that is, an unpitched vessel]. It becomes exceptionally white. Every resin must first be strained while being melted, however, so that its dirt is separated out.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.71.5, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

Section 5 of the long chapter on the terebinth and resins (1.71). Wellmann prints the section number 5 inside the first sentence (ἐν τετραπλασίονι ἀγγείῳ 5 κατὰ …); the cut begins at the sentence boundary, καίεται δὲ πᾶσα ῥητίνη ὑγρὰ, and ends before section 6 (καίονται δὲ καὶ δίχα ὕδατος).

The English places the straining at the end ('must first be strained'); the steps put it first.

The Greek keeps Wellmann's bracketed gloss [τουτέστιν ἀπίσσωτον]; the English renders it [that is, an unpitched vessel].

The resin becomes exceptionally white.

The rainwater is listed as a working material (it is boiled off); plain steps 2 and 3 (vessel, charging) are merged into one boiling step.

Former flag, settled by round 3 item 1 (titles as names): title.en is a phrase copied from the approved English ('Every liquid resin is processed by heating'), awkward as a page title; the approved chapter title is 'The Terebinth'
