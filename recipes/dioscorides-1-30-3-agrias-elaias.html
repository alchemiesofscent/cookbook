ἔλαιον ἀγρίας ἐλαίας · Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.30.3

Wild-olive oil

draft

Oil of the wild olive is more astringent than ordinary oil and second best for health. The text gives no method, only that the oil is made from the wild olive, and its uses.

Method

What to aim for

  • It is more astringent than ordinary oil, and ranks second for maintaining health.

Uses

More astringent: for headache in place of rose oil; checks sweating and falling hair; scours away dandruff, achōres, psōra and lepra; applied every day, slows greying.

The text

The approved English of the passage; turn on Greek (top right) to read Wellmann's Greek beside it.

Greek · Wellmann

3 τὸ δὲ ἐκ τῆς ἀγρίας ἐλαίας ἔλαιον στυπτικώτερον, πρὸς μὲν τὴν ἐν ὑγιείᾳ χρῆσιν δεύτερον, κεφαλαλγοῦσι δὲ ἀντὶ ῥοδίνου ἁρμόζει, ἐπέχον καὶ ἱδρῶτας καὶ τρίχας ῥεούσας. ἀποσμήχει δὲ καὶ πίτυρα καὶ ἀχῶρας καὶ ψώρας καὶ λέπρας, βράδιόν τε πολιοῖ καθ᾿ ἑκάστην ἡμέραν ἐπαλειφόμενον.

English · approved translation

3. Oil from the wild olive is more astringent. Although it ranks second for maintaining health, it is suitable for people with headache in place of rose oil, and it checks both perspiration and falling hair. It also scours away dandruff, achōres, psōra, and lepra, and, when applied every day, slows the graying of the hair.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.30.3, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The first part of section 1.30.3, from 3 τὸ δὲ ἐκ τῆς ἀγρίας ἐλαίας to ἐπαλειφόμενον; the rest of section 3 begins the whitened oil (dioscorides-1-30-whitened-oil).
