σουσῖνον sousinon

Lily oil

Lily perfume family17 ingredientsTime · steep the lilies, rest

g of ἐλαίου (oil) as written

Lily oil is made by steeping lily petals in oil first made astringent with calamus, myrrh and cardamom. The petals of a thousand lilies are steeped in it for a day and a night and pressed, the oil is separated at once from the water pressed out with it and decanted with salt, and the same petals are pressed twice more with more oil and cardamom. Dioscorides ranks the three expressions in that order, says fresh lilies steeped in the first make it stronger, and finishes each preparation with myrrh, cinnamon and saffron.

Time

Steep the lilies
a day and a night; press in the morning
Rest
a short while, before the second pressing; the third is done the same way

The boil and the steeping with cardamom have no stated times. No temperatures are given.

Method

  1. Make the astringed oil Mix 9 lb 5 oz (3–3.1 kg) of oil, 5 lb 10 oz (1.9 kg) of calamus and 5 drachmai (17 g) of myrrh with fragrant wine.

    Boil the mixture.

    Strain off the oil.

  2. Steep the cardamom Chop 3 lb 6 oz (1.1–1.2 kg) of cardamom and soak it in rainwater.

    Pour the strained oil over the cardamom and let it steep.

    Press out the oil.

    The astringed oil This is the oil the lilies are steeped in; saffron oil (1.54) starts from it too.

  3. Steep the lilies Strip the petals from a thousand lilies and lay them in a broad, shallow basin.

    Pour 3½ lb (1.1–1.2 kg) of the astringed oil over them and stir with hands coated with honey.

    Leave them to steep for a day and a night.

  4. Press, separate, decant Put the petals in a straining basket in the morning and press them out.

    Separate the oil at once from the water pressed out with it.

    Decant the oil several times into vessels coated with honey, sprinkling on fine salt and skimming off the impurities that collect.

    Why at once Dioscorides says that, unlike rose oil, lily oil does not tolerate the water: warmed with it, it bubbles up and spoils.

  5. Press the petals again Return the pressed petals from the basket to the basin.

    Pour on the same amount of aromatised oil as before and add 10 holkai (34 g) of chopped cardamom.

    Work it well with your hands, wait a short while, and press it out, skimming what runs off.

    Repeat with oil a third time, adding cardamom and salt again, coating your hands with honey, and pressing.

    Holkai The holke is the drachma weight: 10 holkai are 10 drachmas.

    Grades Dioscorides ranks the first expression finest, the second next, the third last.

  6. A stronger oil Strip another thousand lilies and spread out the petals.

    Pour the first expression over them, work them as the first lilies with cardamom, and press them out.

    Treat the second and third expressions the same way, with cardamom.

    Two kinds of repeat Step 5 presses the same petals again for the second and third expressions; step 6 steeps fresh lilies in them, which Dioscorides says makes the perfume stronger each time.

  7. Finish with spices Mix 2 drachmai (6.7–6.9 g) of good myrrh, 2 drachmai (6.7–6.9 g) of cinnamon and 10 drachmai (34 g) of saffron into each preparation once it is strong enough.

Other ways

A different finish Others pound and sift equal amounts of saffron and cinnamon and put them in the basin with water. They pour the first expression over them, leave it a short while, and take it up into dry vessels coated beforehand with gum, myrrh, saffron, and honey thinned with water. They do the same with the second and third expressions.

No amounts are given; the gum, myrrh, saffron and honey only coat the vessels.

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides ranks the first expression finest, the next second, the last third.
  • According to Dioscorides, the more often fresh lilies are steeped in it, the stronger the perfume.
  • Dioscorides warns that oil left with the water pressed out with it bubbles up when warmed and spoils.
