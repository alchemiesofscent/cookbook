Kyphi

draft

g of raisins the written batch

Kyphi is an incense, not an oil: pleasing to the gods, and much used by the priests in Egypt. Raisins are ground with aged wine and myrrh, mixed with the other ingredients pounded and sifted, and left for a day. Honey boiled until glue-like is mixed with melted resin, the rest is worked in, and the kyphi is stored in an earthenware vessel.

TimeSteep one day, everything togetherThe honey is boiled until it is glue-like; no other times and no temperatures are given.

Method

  1. 1

    Make the raisin paste. Remove the seeds from 12 mnai of plump raisins. Chop them and grind them smooth with 9 xestai of aged wine and 12 drachmas of myrrh.

  2. 2

    Add the other ingredients. Pound and sift the other ingredients and mix them into the raisin paste.

    The other ingredients

    The text does not list them. The resin and honey are dealt with later, so this means the cyperus, juniper berries, aromatic reed, aspalathos and rush.

  3. 3

    Steep. Leave everything to steep together for one day.

  4. 4

    Boil the honey with the resin. Boil 2 mnai of honey until it is glue-like, and carefully mix in the 5 mnai of purified resin, melted.

  5. 5

    Combine and store. Rub the rest of the mixture thoroughly together with the honey and resin, and store it in an earthenware vessel.

Uses

An incense pleasing to the gods, used in abundance by the priests in Egypt; also mixed into antidotes, and given in drinks to those with asthma.

English · approved translation

Kyphi is an incense preparation pleasing to the gods, and the priests in Egypt use it in abundance. It is also mixed into antidotes and given in drinks to those with asthma. Several recipes for it are current, including the following: half a xestes of cyperus; the same quantity of large juniper berries; twelve mnai of plump raisins with their seeds removed; five mnai of purified resin; one mna each of aromatic reed, aspalathos, and rush; twelve drachmai of myrrh; nine xestai of aged wine; and two mnai of honey.

Remove the seeds from the raisins, chop them, and grind them smooth with the wine and myrrh. Pound and sift the other ingredients, mix them with this paste, and leave everything to steep together for one day. Then boil the honey to a glue-like consistency and carefully mix in the melted resin. Finally, thoroughly rub the remaining mixture together with these and store it in an earthenware vessel.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.25.1-2, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

Kyphi is an incense (θυμίαμα), not an oil. Dioscorides gives it as one of several current recipes (ἐν αἷς ἐστι καὶ αὕτη).

The opening on its use (pleasing to the gods, used by the Egyptian priests, mixed into antidotes, given to asthmatics in drink) stands in this chapter and stays with the recipe.

Wellmann prints the section number 2 inside a sentence (ἐκγιγαρτίσας τὴν σταφίδα 2 κόψον); the whole chapter is one recipe, so nothing is cut.

Reader pass: the nine plain steps are grouped into five; the honey and resin steps are merged.
