μυρσίνινον myrsininon

Myrtle oil

8 ingredientsTime · warm

Myrtle oil is made from the juice of tender black-myrtle leaves and unripe-olive oil. The leaves are pounded and pressed, and the juice is warmed over coals with an equal amount of the oil until the two have boiled together, skimmed as it goes. Dioscorides also gives an easier way, boiling the leaves with water and oil, and a homemade one, steeping them in oil in the sun.

Method

  1. Press the juice Pound the tender leaves of black myrtle, wild or cultivated, and press out the juice.

  2. Warm with the oil Mix the juice with an equal amount of unripe-olive oil and warm it over coals until the two have boiled together, skimming off what rises.

    An equal amount As much oil as there is juice.

Other ways

An easier way Pound the tender leaves, boil them with water and oil, and skim off what rises.

An ordinary way Put the leaves into oil and let them steep in the sun.

Ordinary: the Greek ἰδιωτικόν, a plain, homemade method. No time is given.

A prepared oil Some first make the oil astringent with pomegranate rinds, cypress, cyperus and schoinos.

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides finds the more effective oil somewhat bitter and oily to the taste, green, translucent and smelling of myrtle.
