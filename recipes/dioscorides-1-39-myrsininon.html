Myrtle oil

draft

Tender myrtle leaves are pounded and pressed for their juice. The juice is warmed over coals with an equal amount of unripe-olive oil until the two have boiled together. Easier ways are to boil the leaves with water and oil, or to steep them in oil in the sun.

Method

  1. 1

    Press the juice. Take the tender leaves of black myrtle, wild or cultivated. Pound them and press out the juice.

  2. 2

    Warm with the oil. Mix the juice with an equal amount of unripe-olive oil. Warm it over coals until the two have boiled together, removing what rises to the surface.

    An equal amount

    No weights are given: as much oil as there is juice.

Other ways

An easier way. Pound the tender leaves, boil them with water and oil, and skim off what rises.

An ordinary way. Put the leaves into oil and let them steep in the sun.

Ordinary: the Greek ἰδιωτικόν, a plain, homemade method. No time is given.

A prepared oil. Some first make the oil astringent with pomegranate rinds, cypress, cyperus and schoinos.

What to aim for

  • The more effective oil is somewhat bitter and oily to the taste, green, translucent, and smells of myrtle.

Uses

Astringent and hardening: mixed into cicatrizing remedies; for burns, scaly affections, dandruff, eruptions, chafing sores, fissures, knobby growths, loosened joints, sweating, and whatever needs astringency or contraction.

Greek · Wellmann

μυρσίνινον δὲ σκευάζεται οὕτως· λαβὼν τὰ ἁπαλὰ φύλλα τῆς μελαίνης μυρσίνης ἀγρίας ἢ ἡμέρου κόπτε πιέζων· ἴσον δὲ τῷ χυλῷ ἐλαίου ὀμφακίνου μίσγων χλίαινε ἐπ᾿ ἀνθράκων, ἕως ἂν συνεψηθῇ, αἴρων τὸ ἐφιστάμενον. εὐκοπωτέρα δὲ σκευασία· τὰ ἁπαλὰ τῶν φύλλων κόψαντα ἕψειν μετὰ ὕδατος καὶ ἐλαίου καὶ τὸ ἐφιστάμενον ἀποψᾶν. ἰδιωτικὸν δὲ τὰ φύλλα καθέντα εἰς ἔλαιον ἐν ἡλίῳ ἀποβρέχειν· ἔνιοι δὲ προστύφουσι τὸ ἔλαιον σιδίοις, κυπαρίσσῳ, κυπέρῳ, σχοίνῳ. 2 ἔστι δὲ ἐμπρακτότερον τὸ τῇ γεύσει πικρίζον καὶ ἐλαιῶδες, χλωρόν, διαφανές, ὄζον μυρσίνης. δύναμις δὲ αὐτοῦ στυπτική, σκληρυντική· διὸ καὶ ποιεῖ. μειγνύμενον ἀπουλωτικαῖς καὶ πρὸς πυρίκαυτα, ἀχῶρας, πίτυρα, ἐξανθήματα, παρατρίμματα, ῥαγάδας, κονδυλώματα, ἄρθρα κεχαλασμένα, ἱδρῶτας καὶ ὅσα στύψεως ἢ πυκνώσεως ἔχει χρείαν.

English · approved translation

Myrtle oil is prepared as follows. Take the tender leaves of black myrtle, either wild or cultivated, pound them, and press them. Mix unripe-olive oil equal in quantity to the juice and warm it over coals until the two have boiled together, removing what rises to the surface. An easier preparation is to pound the tender leaves, boil them with water and oil, and skim off what rises. An ordinary method is to put the leaves into oil and let them steep in the sun. Some first make the oil astringent with pomegranate rinds, cypress, cyperus, and rush.

The more effective oil is somewhat bitter and oily to the taste, green, translucent, and fragrant with myrtle. It has an astringent and hardening capacity. Consequently, it is mixed with cicatrizing remedies and is also effective for burns, scaly affections, dandruff, eruptions, chafing sores, fissures, knobby growths, loosened joints, sweating, and everything that requires astringency or contraction.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.39, ed. Wellmann.

Editor's notes

The two other methods the text gives, an easier one (εὐκοπωτέρα σκευασία) and an ordinary, homemade one (ἰδιωτικόν), are under Other ways; their ingredients are listed as alternatives.

Some first make the oil astringent with pomegranate rinds, cypress, cyperus and rush.

The juice pressed from the leaves (τῷ χυλῷ) is the recipe's own intermediate product and is not listed as an ingredient.

The more effective oil is somewhat bitter and oily to the taste, green, translucent and fragrant with myrtle.

Wellmann's section number 2 falls at the start of the passage on quality and uses; the whole chapter is one recipe, so nothing is cut.

The myrtle leaves of the two other ways (φύλλων, φύλλα) are folded into the one myrtle-leaf ingredient, and the oil of the ordinary way (ἔλαιον) into the plain-oil item of the easier way; none has an amount of its own.
