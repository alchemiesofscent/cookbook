Saffron unguent

draft · Saffron unguent

g of prepared oil the written batch

Saffron is steeped for five days in oil made astringent as for lily oil, stirred several times a day, and strained off on the sixth. The same saffron is steeped in as much oil again for ten days, and pounded myrrh is stirred in with a mortar at the end.

Method

  1. 1

    Prepare the oil. Make the oil astringent as for lily oil, in the same quantity and the same proportions by weight.

    The astringent oil

    In 1.52: oil boiled with calamus and myrrh in fragrant wine, strained, then steeped with soaked cardamom and pressed.

  2. 2

    Steep the saffron. Put 50 drachmas of saffron into 3½ lb of the astringent oil. Stir it several times a day for five days running.

  3. 3

    Strain. On the sixth day strain the oil cleanly off the saffron.

  4. 4

    A second steeping. Pour the same amount of oil over the same saffron and stir for ten days.

  5. 5

    Finish with myrrh. Pour off the oil, mix in 40 drachmas of pounded, sifted myrrh, stirring it thoroughly in a mortar, and put it away.

    Which oil

    The text does not say whether the myrrh goes into the second oil only or into both.

Other ways

With the henna-oil base. Some use the oil aromatised for henna oil (kyprinon, 1.55) for saffron unguent instead.

What to aim for

  • The best smells intensely of saffron; this is the kind for medical use.
  • The second best has a perceptible scent of myrrh.

Uses

Heating and sleep-inducing: for phrenitis, in soaked compresses or inhaled and smeared on the nostrils; it promotes suppuration and cleanses ulcers; with wax, saffron, marrow and twice as much oil, for indurations, closures and malignant conditions of the uterus; with water, for blue-grey opacities of the eyes.

Greek · Wellmann

κρόκινον δὲ σκευάζων προστύψεις τὸ ἔλαιον, ὡς εἴρηται ἐπὶ τοῦ σουσίνου, πλήθει καὶ συσταθμίᾳ τῇ αὐτῇ. λαβὼν δὲ ἐκ τοῦ ἐστυμμένου ἐλαίου εἰς τὸ σούσινον λίτρας τρεῖς ἥμισυ βάλε κρόκου δραχμὰς πεντήκοντα καὶ ἀνακίνει πλεονάκις τῆς ἡμέρας, ποιῶν τοῦτο συνεχῶς ἐπὶ ἡμέρας πέντε. ἀπήθει δὲ τὸ ἔλαιον τῇ ἕκτῃ ἀπὸ τοῦ κρόκου καθαρίως, τῷ δὲ αὐτῷ κρόκῳ ἐπίχει τὸ ἴσον πλῆθος τοῦ ἐλαίου καὶ ἀνακίνει ἡμέρας δέκα, εἶτα ἀποχέας πρόσμειξον σμύρνης κεκομμένης καὶ σεσησμένης δραχμὰς τεσσαράκοντα καὶ κίνει ἐν ὅλμῳ χρηδίμως, εἶτα ἀπόθου. ἔνιοι δὲ τῷ εἰς τὸ κύπρινον ἠρωματισμένῳ ἐλαίῳ καὶ εἰς τὸ κρόκινον χρῶνται. ἔστι δὲ αὐτοῦ κράτιστον τὸ κατακόρως κρόκου πνέον, ὅπερ εἰς τὴν ἰατρικὴν χρῆσιν ἁρμόζει, δεύτερον δὲ τὸ σμύρνης παρεμφαῖνον. 2 δύναμιν δὲ ἔχει θερμαντικήν, ὑπνωτικήν, ὅθεν πολλάκις ἐπὶ τῶν φρενιτικῶν ἥρμοσεν ἐν καταβροχαῖς ἢ ἀποσφρανθὲν καὶ τῶν μυξωτήρων διαχρισθέντων. ἐστι δὲ καὶ πυοποιὸν καὶ ἑλκῶν καθαρτικόν, καὶ πρὸς τὰς ἐν τῇ μήτρᾳ σκληρίας καὶ μύσεις καὶ τὰ ἐν αὐτῇ κακοήθη σὺν κηρῷ καὶ κρόκῳ καὶ μυελῷ καὶ ἐλαίῳ διπλασίονι· συμπέττει γὰρ καὶ μαλάττει καὶ πραύνει καὶ ὑγραίνει· ποιεῖ καὶ πρὸς τὰς ἀπογλαυκώσεις μεθʼ ὕδατος ὑπαλειφόμενον. ἀναλογεῖ δὲ αὐτῷ καὶ τὸ λεγόμενον ὀνύχινον καὶ βουτύρινον καὶ στυράκινον, τοῖς ὀνόμασι μόνον διαφέροντα, τῇ μέντοι σκευασίᾳ καὶ τῇ δυνάμει παρόμοια ὄντα.

English · approved translation

To prepare saffron unguent, first make the oil astringent, as stated for susinum, using the same quantity and the same proportion by weight. Take three and a half litrai of the oil made astringent for lily unguent, add fifty drachmai of saffron, and stir it several times each day for five consecutive days. On the sixth day, strain the oil cleanly from the saffron. Pour the same quantity of oil over that same saffron and stir for ten days. Then pour off the oil, mix in forty drachmai of myrrh that has been pounded and sifted, stir it thoroughly in a mortar, and put it away. Some also use oil perfumed for henna unguent in making saffron unguent. The best kind is intensely fragrant with saffron and is the one suited to medical use; the second-best has a perceptible scent of myrrh.

2. It has a heating and sleep-inducing capacity. It has therefore often proved suitable for patients with phrenitis, either applied in soaked compresses or inhaled and smeared on the nostrils. It also promotes suppuration and cleanses ulcers. It is used for indurations and closures in the uterus and for malignant conditions there, together with wax, saffron, marrow, and twice as much oil; for it brings to coction, softens, soothes, and moistens. When applied with water, it is also effective against blue-gray opacities of the eyes. The unguents called onychinon, boutyrinon, and styrakinon correspond to it: although their names differ, they are similar in preparation and capacity.

Source

Dioscorides, De materia medica 1.54, ed. Wellmann.
