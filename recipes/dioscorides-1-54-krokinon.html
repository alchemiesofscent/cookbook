κρόκινον krokinon

Saffron unguent

Saffron unguent family5 ingredientsTime · steep, strain, steep again

g of ἐλαίου (Prepared oil) as written

Saffron unguent is made from saffron steeped in oil made astringent as for lily oil (1.52). The saffron is steeped for five days, stirred several times a day, and strained off on the sixth; the same saffron is steeped for ten days in as much oil again, and pounded myrrh is stirred into the oil in a mortar. Dioscorides ranks first the oil that smells strongly of saffron, the kind for medical use, and second the one with a scent of myrrh.

Method

  1. Prepare the oil Make the oil astringent as for lily oil (1.52), in the same quantity and the same proportions by weight.

    The astringed oil Oil boiled with calamus and myrrh in fragrant wine, strained, then steeped with soaked cardamom and pressed (1.52).

  2. Steep the saffron Steep 50 drachmai (168–172 g) of saffron in 3½ lb (1.1–1.2 kg) of the astringed oil for five days, stirring several times a day.

  3. Strain Strain the oil cleanly off the saffron on the sixth day.

  4. A second steeping Steep the same saffron in the same amount of oil again for ten days, stirring.

  5. Finish with myrrh Pour off the oil.

    Stir in 40 drachmai (134–137 g) of pounded, sifted myrrh, working it thoroughly in a mortar.

    Store it.

Other ways

With the henna-oil base Some use the oil aromatised for henna oil (kyprinon, 1.55) for saffron unguent instead.

What to aim for

  • Dioscorides ranks first the oil that smells intensely of saffron, and says it is the kind for medical use.
  • Dioscorides ranks second the oil with a perceptible scent of myrrh.

Source
